What this is, and how it was checked

  
The workbench reads a model written in the language of Dynare, finds its steady state,
  takes a first-order approximation around it with exact derivatives, checks the Blanchard–Kahn conditions and solves
  for the decision rule; on request it adds the second-order terms, with which risk moves the means and a response
  depends on the size and sign of its shock. The numerical work is done by Cozy, a small
  matrix language, and its dsge package, compiled to WebAssembly and running in this page. Nothing is sent
  to a server.

  
Each example model was also solved by Dynare 6.0. The workbench's steady state, decision rule, eigenvalues, impulse
  responses, standard deviations, correlations, autocorrelations and variance decomposition agree with Dynare's to
  better than one part in a billion on nine of the ten, and to one part in a million on the badly conditioned
  Fernandez-Villaverde model, where the workbench's solution is the one that satisfies the model's equations more
  closely. To second order the terms of the decision rule, the means and a pruned simulation are compared with Dynare's
  stoch_simul(order=2); a model declared linear has no such terms. The numbers below are the largest
  difference, relative to the size of the quantity. The two models with an occasionally binding constraint are also
  simulated by Dynare's OccBin for the surprise shocks in their files: the constraint must bind in the same periods,
  and the paths are compared. The comparison runs on every change to the code.

  

  
Limits. No third order; no estimation, no optimal policy; one occasionally binding
  constraint, not two. Variances are those of the first-order solution, and of the unfiltered model. Statements the workbench does not use are listed under the editor
  instead of being silently dropped.
